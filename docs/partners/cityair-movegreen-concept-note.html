A waste-and-methane pilot: validating satellite alerts with your ground network

One page, no confidential content, no commitment. This note exists to answer one question: is a small joint pilot worth a 45-minute call?
1 pilot district 90 days open method your network stays yours status: draft — nothing agreed

1 · The idea in three sentences

Satellite and thermal alerts (NASA FIRMS, Sentinel-2) already flag burning and hotspots around Bishkek, but nobody has published how often those alerts are actually right. MoveGreen has something almost nobody in the region has: a real ground network and public trust. Pairing the two produces a defensible answer for the city — and a method other cities can copy.

2 · What the pilot would produce in 90 days

Validation noteHow many satellite alerts matched a real ground event — including the false-positive rate, published rather than hidden.
Hotspot registerWaste-burning and landfill-risk sites in one district, each entry with how it was detected, how it was checked and who owns the site.
Baseline statementMeasured, modelled and unknown kept apart. Methane is handled as a measurement plan, never as a satellite number presented as quantification.

3 · Division of work

MoveGreen brings

  • The ground monitoring network and its data
  • Local knowledge of sites, routes and risks
  • Citizen engagement and public communication
  • Convening power with the city and the region

CityAir brings

  • The method: satellite screening, validation, confidence scoring
  • Publishing layer: dashboard, trilingual materials, field-report tool
  • The 12-domain readiness assessment, run with you and the city
  • Open method and open data, so others can reproduce it

4 · What we are asking for

  1. One pilot district in Bishkek and one named focal point.
  2. Historical PM data for that district, in whatever granularity you are comfortable sharing.
  3. One introduction to a waste-management, landfill or university partner.
  4. Agreement in writing on what may be published — MoveGreen holds veto over anything describing its own network.
  5. Roughly two hours a week of one person's time during the pilot.

5 · Data, privacy and publication rules

  • Your data stays yours. Nothing is published or redistributed without written agreement.
  • Anything presented as fact carries its method and its limits; uncertainty is stated, not smoothed over.
  • No partnership is announced until there is a published output both sides have seen.
  • If either side wants to stop, the pilot stops and the joint material is withdrawn on request.

6 · Where this fits

The waste workstream sits on top of existing international guidance (the AQMx platform's waste-management and open waste-burning guidance) and is designed to have one documented pilot result before COP31 in Antalya (9–20 November 2026). CityAir is an independent prototype built by the GreenHope Initiative; it is not an AQMx product and does not speak for any organisation listed here.
